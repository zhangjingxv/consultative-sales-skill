# Move a quote toward an order

Help the customer make a suitable purchase while making the working relationship easier. Friendly conversations, a useful meeting, and receipt of a quote are different signals. None establishes that an order is underway.

## Map the actual buying chain

An ICT sale may involve an end customer, a systems integrator, a reseller or distributor, and a manufacturer. Identify the roles in this transaction rather than inferring authority from a job title or company name.

| Role to identify | What to establish | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| User | Operating organization, workload, intended outcome | Confirmed use and requirements |
| Evaluator | Technical, security, and business reviewers | Review results and open issues |
| Order and contract owner | Who initiates procurement; the purchasing and selling entities | Buying process and named counterparties |
| Payer | Paying entity, budget source, agreed payment milestones | Payment terms and approval route |
| Service owner | Responsibility for supply, installation, integration, warranty, and support | Agreed delivery and service scope |

One organization can hold several roles, and one contact may own only a single step. The end user may not purchase from or pay you. A manufacturer warranty does not establish who will install the equipment.

A possible chain is: the end customer defines the outcome, the integrator coordinates delivery, the reseller buys upstream, and the manufacturer supplies its contracted product and support. Verify the actual division of work for each deal.

Resolve one missing fact with the current contact. Do not approach their customer or manager without permission:

> To put this quote into the right purchasing process, will your company place the order, or will the end customer buy directly?

For resale transactions, record both your buyer and the ultimate user. An upstream supply condition is not automatically an accepted downstream term.

## Find the current blocker after quoting

Establish what prevents the next step: unresolved fit, unclear value, insufficient budget, an unfinished procurement step, or no active need. Work on the most consequential issue in this conversation while keeping other unknowns visible.

| Record one current issue | Make it concrete |
|---|---|
| Blocker | A problem the customer recognizes, not a guessed objection |
| Closure condition | The evidence, validation, or approval that resolves it |
| Actions | What you will provide and who will review it |
| Timing and evidence | An agreed date and an observable completion result |
| Next buying step | A purchase request, contract review, or order confirmation |

“We will think about it” and “I mentioned it to my manager” do not define closure. Try:

> Is the remaining issue the service scope? I can separate installation from warranty coverage. Once that is confirmed, we can move to the purchase request.

If the customer identifies a different blocker, update your understanding. Do not choose the issue merely because it is the easiest one for you to answer.

## Ask for a buying step when the conditions are met

Once the necessary technical, pricing, delivery, and purchasing conditions are confirmed, propose one concrete action. A good relationship does not require indefinite waiting.

> This version reflects the points you confirmed. Could procurement start the purchase request using it? I can supply the quote and supporting documents.

> If the contract terms are now approved, who will initiate the order? I can prepare the documents they need.

The authorized buyer still decides. Until they agree, record “next step proposed, awaiting response.” Silence and “that should be fine” are not orders.

If a necessary condition cannot be met, explain the gap and viable alternatives. If there is no buying plan, defer the opportunity and agree when a meaningful change would justify reconnecting.

## Synthetic scenario 1: technical approval without a purchase request

An IT lead confirms that two servers meet the agreed requirements. Budget has been allocated, but procurement has not received a complete package. The seller and contact speak every week without a buying action.

- Roles: the end customer uses, evaluates, contracts, and pays; the seller supplies equipment; installation and warranty follow the agreed scope.
- Blocker: the purchase request lacks a service-scope attachment. Technical support does not establish procurement acceptance.
- Closure: the IT lead approves the attachment, and procurement confirms the package is complete.
- Buying step: “The attachment is confirmed. Could you send the package to procurement today to start the request? I can fill any remaining document gaps.”

Use “today” only when it reflects an agreed date. Completion means the request was accepted or the next formal step was identified; personal support alone is insufficient.

## Synthetic scenario 2: an integrator buys for an end customer

An integrator purchases switches from the seller for a larger project. The end customer's network team evaluates and accepts the solution. The manufacturer supplies agreed warranty support; the seller has not committed to software integration.

- Roles: the end customer uses and evaluates; the integrator orders, contracts, and pays the seller; service responsibilities are explicitly agreed.
- Blocker: the integrator is unsure whether on-site integration is included. “We will help with everything” does not resolve the scope.
- Closure: both parties confirm the supply list, quantities, commercial and delivery terms, and service scope; the customer identifies the integration owner.
- Buying step: “The list, quantities, delivery terms and integration responsibilities are confirmed. Could you issue the purchase order against this version? I can prepare the supporting documents.”

The end customer's acceptance schedule does not automatically amend the integrator's payment terms with the seller. The relevant parties must expressly confirm any change.

## Let the relationship continue after delivery

Complete delivery and resolve outstanding issues first. Discuss expansion, renewal, or another purchase in light of actual use, rather than treating frequent chats as evidence of satisfaction.

> This delivery has been accepted. Please contact me if anything needs attention in use. When it suits you, I would be glad to understand whether another batch is planned.

Ask permission for a referral: “If you were happy with how we worked together, an introduction would be welcome when a colleague needs similar equipment.” Do not ask for contact lists, require an endorsement, or make friendship a reason to buy.
