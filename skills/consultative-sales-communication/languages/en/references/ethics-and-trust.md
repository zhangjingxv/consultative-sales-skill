# Understanding customers, earning trust and respecting boundaries

Trust grows through understanding a particular person and showing respect and reliability.
Shared interests are an opening, not the whole relationship. Understand their responsibilities,
real concerns and preferred way of working, then act accordingly.

The seller cannot declare trust on the customer's behalf. Observe whether the customer
volunteers concerns, corrects your understanding or brings real questions, and whether you
keep promises, admit unknowns, update on time and correct mistakes. These are limited
observations, not trust scores, personality judgments or purchase probabilities.

## Turn understanding into reliable cooperation

Learn naturally from the current conversation, not by interrogating every category:

| What to understand | How to respond |
|---|---|
| Work context and responsibility | Understand who and what outcomes they are responsible for without inventing internal politics |
| What matters and what worries them | Check your understanding; distinguish product, delivery and cooperation concerns |
| Communication and decision preferences | Respect stated channels, timing and detail; allow preferences to change with context |
| Voluntarily shared interests and news | Converse genuinely and share truthful experiences; never fabricate similarity or expect a purchase in return |
| Past commitments | Deliver or explain what remains and the next update; do not cover a broken promise with small talk |

Listen and invite correction, take a small reliable action, then update from actual feedback.
If a customer says “Stop sending piles of documents; I worry about acceptance,” establish the
actual acceptance concern and organize the relevant criteria and responsibilities. More case
studies, a hobby diversion or a guarantee of acceptance do not resolve that concern.

If the customer says you misunderstood them, acknowledge the possible error, recap what is
clear and clarify only the gap that affects the next step. When you missed a commitment,
take responsibility and repair it rather than treating dissatisfaction as insufficient familiarity.

## The three goals are not substitutes

- Product value must fit actual requirements. Friendship cannot make an unsupported fit suitable.
- No current need means no manufactured anxiety; the person can still be a valued long-term contact.
- A real need with cooperation concerns calls for understanding and reliable action, not simulated closeness instead of validation.
- Confirmed conditions and willingness to buy call for procurement support, not an extra requirement to become closer first.

Use [customer context](customer-context.md) for permitted material and private notes. Without
history, start honestly from this conversation rather than pretending you already know the person.

## Integrity and evidence

Use relevant verified evidence. Cases state context, constraints, action, result
and applicability; anonymize identities unless disclosure is authorized.

Replace impression management with evidence, control with a clear agenda,
artificial similarity with accurate understanding, and repetitive business chasing with valid
follow-up triggers. Ordinary contact needs no sales opportunity. Accept mismatch or refer honestly.

Never fabricate resources, relationships, stock, approvals or deadlines; use
shame, obedience tests, deliberate withdrawal, stereotypes or emotional pressure;
hide costs, conditions or obligations; treat silence as consent or refusal as a game.

Distinguish current formal evidence, current written confirmation, reproducible
validation, historical evidence, experience and inference. Weaker evidence can
suggest a candidate, not a promise. Ask whether the customer would consider the
message fair if they knew the facts, gaps and purpose in full.
