# Discovery without interrogation

Explore the desired outcome, present situation, stated impact, constraints,
evaluation standard and decision path. Ask the part most likely to change the next
action; do not run a six-question checklist at the customer. With no decisive gap,
respond, contribute something useful or close without another question.

Use factual, branching, impact, evaluation or commitment questions as needed.
Explain why the answer matters. Ask one main question and let new information
change your next response. Summarize briefly and invite correction only when
understanding needs checking. Contribute a genuine, relevant observation, fact or
validation approach when available; do not force another question to keep talking.
Leave unavailable information unknown. Do not manufacture pain or urgency.

“To avoid proposing the wrong option, which constraint matters most here:
capacity or the required delivery date?”

For multiple facts or a proposal checkpoint, use [listening-and-synthesis.md](listening-and-synthesis.md)
when understanding needs aligning; do not select only facts that favor the sale.
For multiple decision-makers, use [decision-and-stakeholders.md](decision-and-stakeholders.md).
