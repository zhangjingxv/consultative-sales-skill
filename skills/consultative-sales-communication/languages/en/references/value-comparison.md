# Make the value comparison checkable

Help the customer decide whether an added cost is worthwhile and whether the less expensive option meets the need. Align the comparison before presenting differences. This guidance supports sales conversations; it does not replace configuration, compatibility checks, or pricing validation.

## Compare equivalent scope

| Dimension | Align the basis |
|---|---|
| Quantity and capability | Same quantities, workload, capacity, and necessary performance conditions |
| Product and configuration | Models, components, versions, licenses, and required accessories |
| Delivery | Transport, installation, migration, integration, and training included |
| Service | Coverage period, location, response arrangements, and responsible provider |
| Commercial terms | Currency, tax treatment, payment terms, and quote validity |
| Time horizon | Same useful period, workload assumptions, and replacement cycle |

List differences that cannot be aligned and explain their relevance. A supported solution and a bare hardware price are not equivalent offers. Higher specifications do not by themselves establish customer value.

## Give each input a source

Record the value, unit, source, source date, applicable scope, and whether it is confirmed, estimated, or assumed. Sources may include valid quotes, customer work orders, measurements, and a customer-approved costing basis.

An unsourced number may appear in a clearly labeled illustration, but not as a realized customer benefit. Old quotes, another project's results, and manufacturer laboratory measurements need validation before use in this transaction.

| Input | Required basis |
|---|---|
| Acquisition cost | Currency per complete option; included components, taxes, and initial services |
| Operations time | Hours per year; tasks, sample, and observation period |
| Hourly cost | Currency per hour; customer-confirmed cost components |
| Energy | Kilowatts × hours per year × price per kilowatt-hour; load and tariff sources |
| Maintenance and renewals | Currency per year; avoid overlapping warranty, license, and subscription charges |
| Exit cost and residual value | Costs or recoveries at the same point in time; basis and uncertainty |

## Formulas and boundaries

```text
Annual operations labor cost = relevant annual hours × confirmed hourly cost
Period cost = acquisition and implementation
            + maintenance, licenses, energy, and operations costs for each year
            + exit costs − residual value on the same basis
Difference = option A period cost − option B period cost
```

Count each cost once. If an hourly rate already includes an outsourced service charge, do not also add that same charge in full.

List omitted and unknown items; do not treat them as zero. Call a result complete TCO only when the scope supports that description. Otherwise use “subtotal of included costs.” For longer comparisons, agree whether to discount and use a consistent rate.

## A worked example with explicit assumptions

All inputs below are invented assumptions for an illustration created on 2026-10-24. They are not quotes, measurements, or promised benefits. Assume both options meet the same requirements and compare three years without discounting, using the same currency and tax treatment.

| Input | Option A | Option B | Source, unit, and status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Acquisition and implementation | 120,000 | 126,000 | Illustration assumption; CNY per option; replace with valid quotes |
| Relevant operations time | 120 | 80 | Illustration assumption; hours/year; validate with work orders or a pilot |
| Hourly cost | 200 | 200 | Illustration assumption; CNY/hour; customer confirmation needed |
| Comparison period | 3 | 3 | Illustration assumption; years; customer confirmation needed |

Only acquisition, implementation, and the specified operations time are included. Energy, renewals, other maintenance, exit costs, and residual value are omitted. This example does not establish a lower complete TCO.

```text
A three-year included-cost subtotal = 120,000 + 3 × 120 × 200 = CNY 192,000
B three-year included-cost subtotal = 126,000 + 3 ×  80 × 200 = CNY 174,000
Three-year difference = 192,000 − 174,000 = CNY 18,000
Assumed annual hours avoided = 120 − 80 = 40 hours
Labor-cost equivalent = 40 × 200 = CNY 8,000/year
```

Under these assumptions and within this scope, B has an included-cost subtotal CNY 18,000 lower. At only 10 avoided hours per year, the three-year labor-cost equivalent would just offset the CNY 6,000 acquisition premium. Below that level, this factor alone would not offset it.

## Time capacity is not automatically cash savings

If staffing and salaries do not change, fewer hours may provide capacity for other work without reducing cash spending. Do not say that CNY 8,000 will be saved each year unless avoided outsourcing, overtime, or other actual payments are established.

Do not divide a multiyear benefit total by the purchase price and label the result a payback period. Payback requires incremental investment, realizable net cash flows, and their timing. Without those inputs, do not state a payback period.

Do not invent hourly downtime losses, sales growth, or profit improvements. Without relevant customer data and a defensible causal basis, describe an impact to validate rather than adding it to claimed benefits.

## Turn the numbers into a buying decision

Explain the choice, present only the necessary figures, and ask for one useful confirmation:

> Both options are compared against your confirmed requirements. B costs CNY 6,000 more to purchase. If it actually removes 40 hours of repetitive maintenance each year, this illustration puts its three-year included-cost subtotal CNY 18,000 lower. That is an unverified labor-cost equivalent. Could we first check how much time those tasks currently take each year?

Discuss the purchase after validating the inputs. If the customer needs basic capability and the additional value cannot be demonstrated, recommend the adequate lower-cost option. If the difference is established, use the agreed basis to propose the next purchasing step.
