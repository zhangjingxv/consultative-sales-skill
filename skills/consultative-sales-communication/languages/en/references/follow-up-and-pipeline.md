# Follow-up and opportunity health

First recover from the conversation: latest contact boundary, agreed checkpoint,
outstanding seller action, and genuinely new facts. Do not re-ask known facts. Preserve
conflicting claims and clarify only when they affect the current action.

Apply priority by subject, not by assigning one blanket state to the customer:

1. A stop-contact request overrides a new sales reason. A new offer or document does
   not reopen rejected marketing; renewed permission must cover that subject.
2. Existing service, correction and risk duties continue through the agreed channel.
   Send only the necessary update, without a cross-sell. Refusing one product does not cancel service.
3. Keep an agreed update even when there is no progress. State work done, unresolved
   facts and the next update you can own; an update time is not a resolution guarantee.
   An internal reminder is not a mutually agreed checkpoint.
4. Otherwise check new value and authorized triggers. Without either, wait, omit the
   customer-facing draft, and do not invent a three- or seven-day follow-up interval.

Update only the fact the latest message actually changes. A reschedule does not erase
other verified facts. Do not guess a calendar date from “next week” without a dated anchor.

Contact the customer when an agreed date arrives, relevant new evidence appears,
requested work is complete, an authorized condition occurs, or an applicable
commercial change has been verified. Elapsed time or fear of being forgotten
does not create value.

Structure: one previously agreed fact, one new fact/completed action, one easy
next step. With no trigger, wait and record what would justify renewed contact.
Repeated weak replies prompt reassessment rather than a universal attempt count.

Respect the exact scope of a refusal. Stop rejected marketing; keep required
service, correction and delivery updates through agreed channels. Do not switch
channels to resume the same rejected request.

Assess opportunity health from a real problem, decisive facts, evaluation and
approval paths, mutual actions and completed commitments. A friendly relationship
cannot replace missing project evidence.
