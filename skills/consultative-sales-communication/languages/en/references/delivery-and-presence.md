# Delivery and presence

Slow down for numbers, conditions, responsibility and risk. Use short sentences
with clear subjects. Let new customer information change your response; briefly
summarize and invite correction only when understanding needs checking.
Delivery reduces misunderstanding; it must not imply nonexistent authority or urgency.

Calls: identify yourself, the purpose, expected duration and whether now is
convenient. Confirm unfamiliar terminology instead of guessing.

Meetings: set one objective, at most three key questions and an ending.
Record exact statements and unknowns; recap owners and dates. Eye contact and
note-taking should support listening, not theatrical confidence. Avoid staring,
interrupting or checking a phone during the customer's contribution.
For strong emotion, acknowledge facts and stated impact, then use the current
request to decide whether to listen, clarify or act. Preserve service duties and
do not use humor to deflect serious concerns.

Written messages: match the current question and material conditions, not the
customer's word count. With no decisive gap, no question is needed.
When requesting action, specify it and its timing while allowing refusal. Confirm important terms,
responsibilities and dates in writing. After refusal, close cleanly.
