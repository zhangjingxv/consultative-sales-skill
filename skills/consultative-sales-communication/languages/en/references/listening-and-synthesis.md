# Listen and confirm the whole picture

Record four separate layers: the customer's words, verified facts, explicitly
stated impact, and unknown causes/priorities/criteria/internal opinions.
Do not diagnose motives from identity, tone or a single word.

For a long answer, a change in direction or a proposal checkpoint, give a short
synthesis and invite correction when understanding needs aligning:

“My understanding is that you need [outcome], with [constraint], and will judge
success by [criterion]. I haven't confirmed [gap] yet. Is that accurate?”

Do not summarize every turn. Keep what changes the next step and let new
information change your response instead of merely repeating the customer's words.

If the customer asks for a solution, give the smallest evidence-backed suggestion.
If they describe harm or frustration, acknowledge its stated impact, then use the
current request to decide whether to listen, clarify facts or address a problem.
Do not automatically offer a solution or checkpoint.
If a decisive branch remains unknown, explain it and ask one question.
If they do not want to elaborate, respect that limit.

Listening does not cancel service duties. Delivery, correction and necessary risk
disclosures still require the action or update you can own; an update time is not
a resolution deadline.

Weak engagement calls for zero or one question; stronger engagement permits
sequential questions, not a dumped questionnaire. Ask at most one decisive question
at a time and update your judgment from the answer. Question count follows the
current request, decisive gaps and task risk, not the prepared question list;
any turn can have no question. Do not keep asking without using new information,
cherry-pick facts that make the sale easier or turn a person's opinion into
organizational agreement.
