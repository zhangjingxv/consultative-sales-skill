# Customer context and natural contact

Understand the customer, find a topic both people can genuinely discuss, and offer suitable
business help when a real need appears. A conversation can be valuable without producing a lead or order.

## Working with available context

Use customer statements, shared-post text/screenshots supplied by the user, prior interactions,
or material the user explicitly authorizes you to access and the current tools actually expose.
Retain source and date; mark cropped context, uncertain authorship or missing dates as unknown.
Do not claim to have viewed inaccessible WeChat Moments. Work from supplied excerpts when access is unavailable.

Visibility is not permission to publish. Keep real screenshots, names, verbatim chats and customer
records out of the public skill and tests; do not re-upload them to a separate model service by default.
Stay within the specified customer and scope rather than scanning friends or starting ongoing monitoring.

## Choosing a topic

1. Identify the concrete shared fact and whether it is recent and attributable to this person.
2. Check relationship fit: previous conversations, frequency, response, active complaints and contact boundaries.
3. Respond to one detail with sincere interest, congratulations or curiosity. Ask honestly if unfamiliar;
   never invent a shared experience. A friendly statement may be enough without a question.
4. Follow the response. Move to business only when the customer introduces a business issue,
   welcomes help or a genuinely relevant matter exists. Do not append a sales invitation to every exchange.

Prefer voluntarily shared work news, public activities and non-sensitive interests. Do not infer budget,
personality, health, relationship status or buying intent from appearance, spending or family photos.
Do not use vulnerabilities to press for a sale. Respond to sensitive experiences only when the person
currently raises them and welcomes support, rather than reviving them as a sales topic.

## Synthetic examples

**A customer you have discussed running with posts about finishing their first half marathon.**
“Congratulations on your first half! How did the last few kilometers feel?” Or simply congratulate them.
Do not claim to have run the same event unless true. Enthusiasm about running is not purchase intent.

**A customer shares that their office move is complete; no project is known.**
“Congratulations on getting settled into the new office—it sounds like a busy stretch.” Do not presume
they need servers. If they then mention unreliable business systems, ask one useful question, such as
whether access is slow or disconnects frequently. The technical cause remains unverified.

**Product marketing is declined, but discussion of a shared hobby remains welcome.**
Reply within the existing social relationship without a promotion. If the request was to stop all contact,
stop; do not use comments, reactions or alternate accounts to circumvent it.

## Private customer note, when ongoing context is useful

Capture only relevant information, without turning the conversation into a questionnaire:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Person and organizational role | Confirmed identity/responsibilities; keep gaps unknown |
| Current activity | Brief faithful summary, source and date |
| Business concerns | Confirmed needs, constraints, participants and hypotheses to verify |
| Topics and communication preferences | Volunteered or observed preferences and contact boundaries |
| Commitments and feedback | Who agreed to what, update time and actual customer actions |

Update the relevant facts when new replies arrive; do not promote repeated assumptions into facts.
Save only to a user-designated private customer record when needed; avoid unrelated family or location
details. Do not claim cross-conversation memory without saved state. Abstract reusable patterns for public
guidance instead of copying customer statements.

Track relationship outcomes (voluntary two-way exchange, trust, useful context) separately from business
outcomes (confirmed need, validation, purchasing step, order). Reactions, politeness, read receipts and orders differ.
