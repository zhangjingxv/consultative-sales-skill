# Integrity and trust

Trust comes from observable consistency: doing what was agreed, admitting unknowns,
updating on time, correcting mistakes and explaining conditions and risk.
Use relevant verified evidence. Cases state context, constraints, action, result
and applicability; anonymize identities unless disclosure is authorized.

Replace impression management with evidence, control with a clear agenda,
artificial similarity with accurate understanding, and persistence with valid
follow-up triggers. Accept mismatch or refer honestly.

Never fabricate resources, relationships, stock, approvals or deadlines; use
shame, obedience tests, deliberate withdrawal, stereotypes or emotional pressure;
hide costs, conditions or obligations; treat silence as consent or refusal as a game.

Distinguish current formal evidence, current written confirmation, reproducible
validation, historical evidence, experience and inference. Weaker evidence can
suggest a candidate, not a promise. Ask whether the customer would consider the
message fair if they knew the facts, gaps and purpose in full.
